Welcome to the Newark Transit Evolution project. Discover how the historic Morris Canal bed was transformed into the bustling #7 City Subway, and eventually into the modern NJ Transit Light Rail network.

Explore our historical archives and see how vital right-of-way infrastructure continues to shape the growth of Newark and the broader NJ/NYC metropolitan corridor.

Why One Trench Survived Nearly Two Centuries

Most nineteenth-century urban canals vanished completely under highways and office blocks. Newark's section of the Morris Canal survived because its grade-separated right-of-way solved an urgent twentieth-century bottleneck: streetcar congestion at Broad and Market Streets.

By converting the drained waterway into the #7 City Subway in 1935—and later upgrading the tunnel for PCC cars in 1954 and low-floor Kinki Sharyo light rail vehicles in 2001—the corridor avoided the mass rail abandonments that swept American cities after World War II.
